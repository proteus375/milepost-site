For homeschooling families and co-ops

You’re already doing the teaching. Milepost keeps the record.

Attendance, activities, projects and progress — written down as they happen, so the spring paperwork is something you print rather than something you dread.

See how it works Say hello

What it keeps

A year of learning, written down as it happens.

Not one more thing to keep up with. Log the museum trip on the way home, tick the register at the end of the morning, and the record builds itself out of what you were doing anyway.

Activities

Swim club, the museum, the co-op field trip, the hours spent on a model volcano. Photographs attach to the entry, so the evidence and the note live together.

Courses

Build a unit study from pages, readings and questions — or import one somebody sent you — and see which child has finished what.

The week

Lessons, co-op mornings, live classes and the recurring things. Subscribable from whatever calendar you already use.

Attendance

A register per course, per day, with the totals stated and the dated rows kept as the evidence for them.

The portfolio

Everything a child did across whatever span you choose, gathered into one document you can print or hand over — grouped the way an assessor reads it.

The transcript

Completed courses, credit, dates. The document that stops being hypothetical when a sixteen-year-old starts looking at colleges.

When it has to be believed

The transcript is where the care shows.

One day this leaves your house and lands on a stranger’s desk. Milepost tells that stranger exactly what they are holding: which courses a co-op certified, and which you taught and stand behind.

Attested credit is totalled and labelled, and deliberately kept out of the grade average — because a number that mixed the two would travel further than the explanation for it.

Your word is on the document, clearly marked as your word. That is what makes the rest of it credible.

Plainly

Milepost will never tell you that you’re compliant.

Requirements are set state by state and they genuinely disagree — on hours, on subjects, on testing, on whether anyone reviews your record at all. Any software that printed “requirements met” would be making a promise it has no way to keep, to somebody who would reasonably believe it.

So Milepost does the useful thing instead, thoroughly: it keeps a complete, dated record of what your children actually did, in a form you can hand to whoever asks — and it says plainly what that record is.

Two things worth knowing early

No accounts for your children. No hostage-taking of your records.

Your children don’t have logins

A child in Milepost is a record you own and manage — no username, no password, no inbox, nothing for anybody to sign in to. You act on their behalf, and so does their other parent, equally.

There is no child account to be broken into, because there is no child account.

Take everything with you, any time

One archive per child, on demand: every course, submission, mark, attendance record and activity, with the photographs and files, alongside the readable portfolio and transcript.

It works whether or not you’re a subscriber. Your family’s record is yours, and never held to ransom.

For co-ops

Households first, and the co-op around them.

A co-op teacher takes their own register, sets work and issues certificates for the courses they teach. What they cannot do is read a family’s compiled record or export it — that belongs to the household, and the line is enforced rather than promised. A family that leaves takes everything with them.
