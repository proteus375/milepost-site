For the child, not the parent

Ten looks. The record underneath is the same one.

Every child on an account picks their own colours and their own picture. It follows them onto every page that is theirs, and their brother’s choice does not come with it.

{% comment %} Name and blurb sit under the picture rather than over it. Over it they would need a scrim on ten different backgrounds, three of which are pale and one of which is nearly black -- and a scrim would hide the thing the picture is here to show. {% endcomment %}

Where it stops

A theme never reaches a document.

The transcript, the report card and the course descriptions are the same pages whichever theme is set. A unicorn in the corner of a transcript is a page an admissions officer has to look past, and a report card handed to a county reviewer is not the place for a rocket.

And what it can change

Colour, and the paper behind it. Nothing structural.

No type sizes, no spacing, no different arrangement of anything. Every palette is measured against the same contrast floor before it ships, because a colour scheme a seven-year-old picked must not make their own schoolwork harder to read.

Also theirs

And a picture, chosen separately.

Forty-one drawings — dinosaurs, pets, planets, unicorns, things found outdoors — and a child picks one for next to their name. It is a different question from the colours, and a child who wants the Ocean palette and a fox is answering both. Leave it, and they get whatever their theme would have put there.
