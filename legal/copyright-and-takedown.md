## Sending a notice

To report material on Milepost that infringes your copyright, send our designated agent a written
notice including:

1. Your physical or electronic signature, as the owner of the right or someone authorised to act for
   them.
2. Identification of the work you say is infringed.
3. Identification of the material on Milepost you say is infringing, **specific enough for us to find
   it** — the listing name and, where you can give it, the page or section.
4. Your address, telephone number and email address.
5. A statement that you believe in good faith that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner,
   its agent, or the law.
6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate, and — under penalty of perjury — that
   you are authorised to act for the owner.

**Notices that leave out these elements may not be effective.** If yours is incomplete we will say so
rather than ignore it.

## What we do when we receive one

1. **We remove or disable access to the material promptly.**
2. **We tell the publisher** what was removed and why, and give them a copy of the notice.
3. **We tell them they may file a counter-notice**, and how.
4. **We record it** against the publisher's account, for the repeat infringer process below.

## Adaptations of removed material

Milepost lets subscribers adapt a pack and publish the result, and records which pack each adaptation
came from.

When we remove a pack, we **review the listings adapted directly from it** and decide about each one
individually — an adaptation may have replaced the infringing part, or may not contain it at all. We
do not remove them automatically, and we do not leave them automatically. If an adaptation is itself
removed, its own direct adaptations get the same review.

We work one step at a time rather than removing an entire tree, because the people who made
adaptations generally did nothing wrong and their own work should not disappear because of somebody
else's problem.

## Copies already downloaded

Subscribers download packs into their own instance, and their licence to a downloaded pack is normally
permanent.

**A removal for infringement is the exception**, and it has to be: a publisher cannot grant rights they
never held. When we remove a pack on a copyright notice, the licence ends for copies already
downloaded, and we notify the subscribers affected.

The child's *record* is unaffected. Attendance, grades, submitted work and completion records for that
course remain in place, readable and exportable. What ends is the licence to the teaching material.

## Counter-notices

If your material was removed and you believe that was a mistake or a misidentification, send our
designated agent a counter-notice containing:

1. Your physical or electronic signature.
2. Identification of the material and where it was before removal.
3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith it was removed as a result of
   mistake or misidentification.
4. Your name, address and telephone number, and a statement consenting to the jurisdiction of the
   federal district court for your address (or, if outside the United States, for any district where
   we may be found), and that you will accept service from the person who sent the notice.

We forward counter-notices to the original complainant. If they do not file a court action within the
statutory period, we may restore the material. [[TO CONFIRM: 10–14 business days per §512(g);
counsel to confirm the exact window and wording]]

**A counter-notice is a legal statement made under penalty of perjury.** It is not a way to express
disagreement, and it puts your name and address in the hands of the complainant.

## Knowingly false notices

Submitting a notice or counter-notice that misrepresents material as infringing, or as wrongly
removed, can make you liable for damages including costs and legal fees.

## Trademark and other complaints

This process is for copyright. For anything else, use the report button on the listing or the contact
address in the Terms of Service.
